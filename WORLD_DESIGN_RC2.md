# RC2 world research and interpretation

Five existing districts retain their stable stop/discovery IDs and coordinates. Four continuous two-way road links join them in a schematic network. The bypass corridors are game routing, not a surveyed or north-oriented geographic map. Walking and vehicle collision use the same shared road segments; scene chunks stream independently of district labels.

| Connection | Public-place inspiration | In-game treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Kozhikode–Malappuram | Ramanattukara, Kottakkal, Malappuram | market road, tiled shops, Kottakkunnu-inspired park |
| Kozhikode–Wayanad | Thamarassery, Lakkidi, Vythiri, Kalpetta | winding approach, guardrails, plantations, forest ridge |
| Kozhikode–Kannur | Koyilandy, Vadakara, Thalassery, Kannur | coastal road, laterite fort silhouette, Payyambalam-inspired promenade |
| Malappuram–Palakkad | Perinthalmanna, Shoranur, Ottappalam, Palakkad | riverbank, footbridge, paddy terraces, irrigation |

Research consulted 2026-10-04, Kerala Tourism (Government of Kerala):
- https://www.keralatourism.org/wayanad/vythiri-town.php — describes Kozhikode's winding approach to Lakkidi, then Vythiri and Kalpetta; forests and plantations inform the scenery.
- https://www.keralatourism.org/malabar/kottakkal/58 — places Kottakkal near Malappuram on the Tirur route.
- https://www.keralatourism.org/malabar/shoranur/47 — Shoranur on Bharathapuzha and nearby Ottappalam inform the Palakkad riverside.
- https://www.keralatourism.org/malabar/taliparamba/141 — trading town near Kannur, greenery and tiled houses.
- https://www.keralatourism.org/malabar/destination_detailed_view/payyambalam-beach/114 — coastal public landscape inspiration.

All geometry is original procedural interpretation. Local stops are condensed exploration scenes, not an assertion that these landmarks are adjacent in real life. River bridges and forts are stylized silhouettes, not architectural replicas. Roads currently remain at one driveable elevation; Wayanad elevation is conveyed through terrain silhouettes rather than climbing physics.
